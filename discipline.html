How we ride

Discipline

Etiquette, safety and conduct — what makes Road Burners a club worth riding with.

We are a group of like-minded people who love to ride and explore new places. We are:

  • NOT a racing or a stunt group.
  • A group who rides with proper riding gear, documents, licence, insurance and pollution certificate.

We follow discipline to maintain a safe and respectful riding environment. Browse the categories below.

Riding etiquette promotes a positive and safe riding experience for everyone on the road. Respect, cooperation and adherence to traffic laws are essential.

Follow all traffic laws and regulations, including speed limits, traffic signals, and lane markings. This ensures the safety of you and others on the road.

Maintain a consistent and predictable riding style. Signal your intentions with proper hand signals or your motorcycle's turn signals.

Keep a safe following distance behind other vehicles, allowing enough time to react and manoeuvre if necessary. Avoid tailgating.

Regularly check your mirrors to be aware of the traffic behind you. Signal and check your blind spots before changing lanes or making turns.

Use hand signals, nodding, or other recognised gestures to communicate with fellow riders. This helps in group riding situations and enhances overall safety.

Treat other road users — pedestrians, cyclists, and motorists — with respect and courtesy. Avoid aggressive or confrontational behaviour.

When riding in a group, follow the predetermined formation and maintain proper spacing. Use hand signals or intercoms to communicate within the group.

Be mindful of excessive noise from your motorcycle's exhaust. Respect noise regulations and the peace of the community by keeping noise levels within acceptable limits.

If lane sharing or filtering is legal in your jurisdiction, do it cautiously and respectfully. Watch for opening car doors and avoid surprising other drivers.

Minimise your impact on the environment by disposing of waste properly and avoiding littering.

Offer help to fellow riders in need if it is safe to do so. Share knowledge, experiences, and resources within the community.

Strive to improve your riding skills through training, practice, and staying updated on current riding techniques and safety practices.

Riding safety standards are guidelines designed to enhance the safety of motorcyclists and reduce the risk of accidents.

Always wear an ISI/DOT/ECE-approved helmet, protective eyewear, gloves, a durable jacket, long pants, and sturdy boots. This gear provides essential protection in case of a crash.

Regularly inspect and maintain your motorcycle per the manufacturer's recommendations. Pay attention to brakes, tyres, lights, signals, fluids and suspension.

Never ride under the influence of alcohol, drugs, or any substance that impairs your judgment, coordination, or reflexes.

Adhere to posted speed limits and adjust your speed based on road conditions. Excessive speed reduces your ability to react to hazards.

Anticipate potential hazards, maintain a safe following distance, and constantly scan your surroundings. Be prepared to react to unexpected situations.

Wear brightly coloured or reflective clothing. Use your motorcycle's headlights during the day and night, and consider adding additional lighting or reflective elements.

Adjust your riding style to match the road conditions — wet surfaces, gravel, or uneven pavement. Slow down and use caution in challenging conditions.

Avoid distractions while riding, such as using electronic devices or listening to loud music. Stay focused on the road, traffic, and potential hazards.

Fatigue impairs your riding abilities. Take regular breaks to rest, hydrate, and stretch during long rides.

Take part in advanced rider training courses to enhance your riding skills, hazard perception, and emergency manoeuvring abilities.

The Decency & Conduct Code reflects the character of The Road Burners Association. These principles ensure every ride and gathering remains inclusive, considerate, and enjoyable for all members and guests.

  • Speak normally — no ego, shouting, sarcasm, or taunts.
  • Do not pull rank or act superior; everyone is equal here.

  • OBs are coordinators, not service staff.
  • No demands or expectation of special treatment; offer help before you complain.

  • No cutting formation, unnecessary revving, tailgating, sudden lane changes, or drama.
  • Never risk the group's safety just to prove a point.

  • Share feedback or disagreements privately.
  • Do not create friction during rides or in group chats.
  • No gossip or backchannel politics.

  • Arrive on time with complete safety gear.
  • Fuel up and check your motorcycle before every ride.
  • Follow Lead/Sweep instructions without arguments.

  • Help riders who are stuck or facing issues.
  • Look out for everyone's safety.
  • Share relevant information when the group needs it.

  • Your conduct should be consistent — on highways, cafés, resorts, public events, WhatsApp, or Instagram.
  • No loud scenes, disrespect to locals, or behaviour that harms the club's image.
Consequences

If someone repeatedly behaves in a rude, entitled, unsafe, or disruptive manner, they will be asked to sit out future rides or even be expelled from the club. No drama, no debates — it's about safety, harmony, and maintaining the culture.
